Purpose



The group will observe and analyze communication barriers in a buyer-and-seller interaction to determine how these barriers may affect KOOLLKIDS sellers' ability to process orders accurately and efficiently, particularly during break or lunch periods when various sources of noise can interfere with communication. In line with this, the group’s objectives for the process observation are:

To assess which physical noises are the most disruptive in the communication process and to suggest ways to lessen the effect of said noises.


To examine how the customer and vendor respond to and overcome the communication barrier.


To determine how communication barriers affect the accuracy and efficiency of order-taking.

Methods



Data Gathering

The group will gather information directly by observing the communication process between KOOLLKIDS sellers and customers during the scheduled observation periods. The following methods will be used to document the communication process properly:


Video Recording and
Photo Documentation
With permission from the sellers and their customers, the group will use their phones to take pictures and record videos of their interactions during the observation. These recordings will serve as a reference for the group in analyzing the communication process.
Field Observation
The group will directly observe the interactions between the sellers and customers using their senses, particularly sight and hearing, to gather relevant information. Additionally, they will observe the elements of communication, including the sender, receiver, message, channel, feedback, context, effect, and noise or possible communication barriers.
Note Taking
Before the observation, the group will prepare a checklist containing the objectives and communication elements to be observed. This will guide the group in accurately identifying and recording specific actions, responses, and significant events during the observation without adding personal interpretations.


Synthesizing the Process

After the observation, the group will organize and analyze their notes and documentation. They will compare the observations from the allotted two schedules to identify recurring communication patterns, effective communication practices, and possible barriers. This method will help the group connect the gathered observations and prepare the information needed for the communication model.


Presenting a Communication Model

To describe the interaction, the group will use the Interactive Communication Model. This model is suitable as it emphasizes that both the seller and customer actively participate in the interaction by responding one after another to each other through their words, actions, and other cues. Applications such as Canva and ibisPaint X will be used to organize and present the elements observed during the transaction.


Limitations of the Method

The observation has several limitations. First, the short observation period may limit the number and variety of interactions and communication situations that can be observed. Second, the group will focus primarily on verbal oral communication, as nonverbal cues may be easily missed or misinterpreted by the observers. Lastly, the presence of the observers and recording devices may cause participants to feel conscious or self-aware, which may influence their natural behavior and communication during the observation.
